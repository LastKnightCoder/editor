The Forgetting Curve Is a Feature

Why the moment you are about to forget something is the best moment to learn it.

By Mara Ellison · 8 min read

In 1885, Hermann Ebbinghaus spent months memorizing lists of nonsense syllables—DAX, BOK, YAT—and testing how quickly he lost them. The result, now called the forgetting curve, is one of the oldest replicated findings in psychology: most of what we learn fades within a day unless something interrupts the decline.

The usual reading of that curve is pessimistic. Memory leaks; study harder. But the more interesting part of Ebbinghaus's data is what happened when he reviewed. Each review didn't just restore the lost material—it bent the curve, so the next decline was slower than the last.

Why difficulty helps

Later researchers gave this effect a name: desirable difficulty. A review that feels effortless does surprisingly little. A review that happens just as you are about to forget—when retrieval takes real effort—strengthens the memory far more than one done while it is still fresh.

This flips a common intuition. Rereading a chapter the next morning feels productive because everything is familiar. But familiarity is not recall. The sense of fluency is precisely the signal that the review is arriving too early to matter.

Three rules that follow

The first rule is timing: schedule reviews for when something is almost forgotten, not when it was just learned. In practice this means the gaps should feel uncomfortable.

The second is retrieval. Close the book and try to produce the answer before checking. The act of pulling a memory out is itself the exercise; looking at the answer again is mostly spectating.

The third is expansion. Intervals should grow as an item gets easier—one day, then three, then a week, then a month. A fixed weekly review wastes time on what you know and comes too late for what you don't.

None of this requires software, though software makes it easier to stay honest. What it does require is a change in attitude: forgetting is not evidence that studying failed. It is the condition that makes the next review worth doing.
